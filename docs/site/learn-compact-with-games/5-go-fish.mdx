The classic social card game where you trawl for matching cards in your opponent’s deck. Mental poker and MPC-style card shuffling for a simple card game.

## Technical challenges

### Arbitrum finality

Card games tolerate more latency than racing, but each turn still has a
hard moment of truth (a draw, a reveal). Fast finality keeps those
moments from feeling like waiting on the network.

### Card shuffling

Neither player can be trusted to shuffle alone. We walk through how the
deck is produced collaboratively so neither side knows the order until
cards are revealed.

### Mental poker

Mental poker is the broader name for the protocol family that makes the
above work. This section is the conceptual core of the post - what it
guarantees, what it doesn't, and where the implementation lives.

### Two-player today, MPC to scale

Two players is the setting where mental poker is most ergonomic. We sketch
where MPC takes over once you want more players at the table, and what
that costs.

### Private key as a circuit input

A player's secret material gets passed straight into the circuit as a
private input, never to the network. This section is short but worth
spelling out - it's the pattern most readers will reuse.
